Two modems, one phone line

A pair of 1990s external modems, emulated instruction by instruction, calling each other across a simulated telephone line. Everything runs in this browser tab.

What is happening

Each modem here is a complete machine: an 80186 host processor running the control firmware, a TMS320C5x signal processor running the data-pump firmware, plus the audio codec, the serial port and the front-panel lamps. When you press Start call, one modem dials, a small telephone exchange gives it dial tone, decodes the digits and rings the other, and the two modems negotiate the way real ones did: answer tone, capability exchange, line probing, training, and finally data.

Real

  • The processors, instruction for instruction, running the modem's own firmware, unmodified.
  • The handshake, the training, the speed changes and the retrains: nothing here is scripted.
  • The numbers on the Link card: they are read from the signal processor's own memory.

Simulated

  • The phone line, and everything you can do to it: noise, loss, frequency shift, jitter, clicks, cuts.
  • The exchange: dial tone, DTMF decoding, ring, and connecting the two lines.
  • The terminals and the serial cables.

Getting started

  1. Load the firmware. This page contains none. Give it your own copy below; it is decoded in your browser and kept in this browser's local storage. Nothing is uploaded.
    Firmware needed. This page contains no modem firmware. Pick your own SV25.XMD or SV20.XMD (or a .zip containing it); it is decoded here in your browser and kept in its local storage, never uploaded.
  2. Open the Lab and press Power on. Both modems start and receive their setup commands, which you can see in the terminals. Choose a connect speed first if you like; it can only change while the modems are off.
  3. Press Start call. The caller dials, the exchange rings the answerer twice, and the handshake takes 10 to 20 seconds. The lamps and the Link card show it happening.
  4. When CONNECT appears, click a terminal and type, or press Send 32 kB random. Every key goes straight to the modem.

Things to try

  • Slide White noise up and watch SNR fall and the speed step down by itself.
  • Add Frequency shift or Phase jitter: the I/Q dots smear, the modems retrain.
  • Press Click or Cut 1 s and see whether the call survives.
  • Choose 300 — V.21 before Power on. It has no constellation at all, and the spectrum shows two tones instead.
  • Turn Listen on and hear the handshake.
  • Press Save snapshot once connected; Load call later resumes it at once.
  • On a slower computer, set the instruments to off.

Reading the screens

Lamps
On the modem's front panel: TR terminal ready, CD carrier detected, OH off hook, SD and RD data sent and received, MR modem ready, CS clear to send, SYN synchronous mode, ARQ error-correction, HS high speed, AA auto-answer.
Link
What the signal processor measures during the call: receive and transmit speed, signal-to-noise ratio, symbol rate, carrier frequency, and how far the carrier and the symbol clock have drifted, in parts per million.
I / Q
The points the modem sends, plotted by in-phase (I) and quadrature (Q) value. A regular grid of dots means QAM. The number of dots grows with speed.
Spectrum
Which frequencies the modem puts on the line, from 0 Hz upwards, in decibels below full scale.
Signal
The audio samples themselves; the slider sets how much time fits across.
FSK
The slow 300 bit/s modes (V.21, Bell 103) switch between two tones for 0 and 1. With no phase or amplitude symbols there is nothing to plot on I/Q.
